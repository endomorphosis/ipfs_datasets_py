# Storage diagnosis and recovery plan

Training uses a 145,000,000,000-byte accounting cap across four named roots. Admission charges apparent file bytes at every pathname, including hardlink aliases, plus every full nonreleased reservation and the requested new allocation. Filesystem free space is a separate check. The read-only intake observed about 496.5 GB user-available disk space and 220 million free inodes, so additional hardware is not the immediate remedy.

The initial metadata snapshot contained 513 reservation records: 411 released and 102 retained. Full retained claims totaled 48,381,777,216 bytes. The shared-root diagnostic observed 96,911,604,740 apparent bytes; these sequential observations explain a 145.29 GB charge, but are not a simultaneous admission receipt. Repeated hardlink pathnames contributed 31,552,270,383 bytes to that logical inventory. Ordinary `du` deduplicates those aliases and understates the owner's charge. The accounting policy remains unchanged.

The existing closeout API used the admission capacity check before releasing a dead owner's retained claim. When the ledger was over cap, that prevented recovery itself. The opt-in `capacity_policy="administrative_closeout"` now records full pre-closeout accounting and a second fresh census for a projected ledger with only the selected claim released. The new v2 receipt explicitly labels that final accounting as a prewrite projection. Actual postwrite accounting requires a separate observation. The default `require_capacity` path and legacy v1 receipt, normal admission, scheduler, pathname accounting and cap are unchanged.

Closeout still requires the exact prior record hash, a matching attempt directory, complete unaliased regular-file inventory with SHA256 and fsync, absent original owner PID, dead recorded child groups, no unsupported external charges, and unchanged selected and foreign records. A single normal atomic ledger write preserves outputs and records an explicit reconciliation event. It grants no allocation, lease, model or training authority. Over-limit and physical-free shortfalls are recorded honestly; an over-cap ledger continues to reject new work.

Current inert validation passed 68 recovery cases plus eight unchanged owner/accounting regressions. Numerical imports, network, subprocesses and real signals are denied; only the fixture process's existing signal-zero liveness probe is allowed. Temporary files, logs and fixture ledgers stay under the isolated artifact root. These tests do not qualify top-level plugin startup or the full repository suite. Three unchanged legacy owner tests still refer to a 140 GB production cap although the current source policy is 145 GB; they were not silently rewritten or counted as passing.

After independent source and concrete-operation reviews, root executed closeout only for our second failed training run's 200,000,000-byte claim. All 512 other bodies stayed unchanged, including the first run's prior closeout. The 15 failed-run files totaling 61,141 bytes remained intact. The actual ledger has 513 records, with 412 released and 101 retained; full outstanding reservations are now 48,181,777,216 bytes. Neither training nor model execution occurred, no cap changed, and full local ledger backups remain private.

The full pre-closeout census recorded 148,650,554,404 charged bytes, 3,650,554,404 above cap. The second fresh census for the released-record projection recorded 148,783,748,326 bytes, still above cap despite the 200 MB reservation reduction because shared files had grown. The separate actual postwrite census then observed 95,292,200,978 apparent bytes plus 48,181,777,216 full reservation bytes: 143,473,978,194 charged bytes with no additional request. Its accounting headroom was 1,526,021,806 bytes. A new 200 MB request would reduce that observed headroom to 1,326,021,806 bytes. Shared-root content changed by more than 5 GB between the projection and actual observation, so this snapshot is not a guarantee of future admission, and the entire drop cannot be attributed to the 200 MB closeout.

## Actions to restore durable training headroom

1. Review retained historical claims individually through their owning interface. The initial audit found 63 metadata candidates totaling 24,316,777,216 bytes, including our now-closed 200 MB claim. The remaining 62 historical candidates therefore represent 24,116,777,216 bytes. Metadata eligibility is not durability or release approval: each needs fresh liveness, exact current hash/inode and complete durable artifact verification. Do not expire claims merely by age or rewrite the ledger directly.
2. Start that review with reservation `474ee6b5ae784516ae1dab7ca38c8cc6`, the generated-field training attempt `decoder-generated-field-training-20261003/training-r1`. Its retained allocation is 4 GB, while today's observed regular output is 249,979,161 bytes. A prior independent cleanup review records four completed fits, while the intended eighteen-fit comparison remains incomplete. A fresh owner-scoped closeout would release the 4 GB reservation while leaving those outputs charged as ordinary files; it must first reconcile the current complete file manifest with the prior review. Use the current reviewed administrative owner; do not rerun or adopt the historical 140 GB guardian. No historical claim has been released in this round, and no archive publication or model promotion is inferred.
3. Build 5–10 GB of observed accounting headroom after counting the next run's request. A further independent sequential census observed 143,472,502,157 charged bytes and 1,527,497,843 headroom. At that snapshot, an additional 3,672,502,157 bytes of relief would leave 5 GB after a 200 MB training request; the reviewed 4 GB candidate could provide that margin if current prerequisites qualify. This is an operating margin for the shared tree's multi-GB fluctuations, not an increase in the cap. Repeat current owner-compatible censuses with ledger/root/source/configuration fences, then let the unchanged guardian perform its actual admission checks.
4. Use separate recovery designs for the other categories: 25 externally charged claims totaling 14.725 GB, 12 claims without a bound attempt totaling 7.34 GB, and two symlink-bearing attempts totaling 2 GB. Their outputs or external references cannot be authenticated by the present closeout profile; no bulk release is justified by the current metadata.
5. Reduce future source-capture growth. Several captures contribute 4–7 GB each because they include archive weights, proof keys and previous evidence, often through hardlinks. Prefer explicit required source/data closures and immutable references to prior archives. Review every existing capture's owner, retention and consuming manifests before moving or removing it. Preserve trained checkpoints and proof/evidence custody.
6. Coordinate any pytest cleanup with the active test owner. The intake found a live lock PID in `pytest-157`, recent changes in `pytest-156` and `pytest-158`, and `pytest-current` pointing at 158. An absent lock alone does not establish that a tree is disposable. The temporary root identity already matches its recorded inode; no root migration is needed now.

Resume the reviewed closure-fixed temporal-contrast plan only after these capacity prerequisites. A successful fit must still retain both checkpoints and all diagnostic panels. Storage recovery supplies no legal-fidelity, latent-dimension, logic-family or compiler qualification.
